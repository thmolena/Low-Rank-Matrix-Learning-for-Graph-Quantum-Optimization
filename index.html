Abstract

A quantum computer is a stochastic device: the Born rule returns one bitstring per run, so every quantity a simulation reports—fidelities, observables, and the very error one is trying to control—is a statistical estimate over samples, and the structure that governs those estimates is the algebra of the operators being evolved. We introduce commutator-graph scheduling (CGS), which doubles the convergence order of first-order Trotterization at zero gate, qubit, or depth overhead by making the leading error an explicit, estimable operator on that algebra. CGS recasts the leading error as an exact, matrix-free operator—the commutator error form Eπ = ∑a<b [hπ(a), hπ(b)], a signed sum of Pauli strings over the edges of the commutator graph, validated against dense exponentials to 10−9. We prove that reordering only re-signs this operator, leaving its norm exactly invariant on collision-free families, so no fixed ordering escapes the O(t²/r) rate; the true free parameter is the per-step schedule, and a step-alternating antithetic schedule cancels the leading error and converges at O(t³/r²) with the identical L·r rotations. Across 144 structured Hamiltonians versus exact statevector references, CGS lifts the empirical convergence rate from 1.96 to 4.07, reaches fidelity 0.99 with 1.40× fewer rotations than the best first-order ordering, and attains 0.999 where first-order Trotter cannot. A matrix-free Krylov reference extends the exact benchmark to 14 qubits, where the order doubling is size independent and the matched-cost speedup reaches 2.06×. An interpretable scheduler over ten graph features selects the gate-optimal schedule per instance with 93% leave-one-out accuracy.

Contributions

  1. An exact, estimable error operator. The commutator error form: a matrix-free representation of the leading Trotter error as a signed Pauli-string sum over the commutator graph (Definition 1), validated against the dense pair-commutator sum to 10−9.
  2. A statistical theory of scheduling, with full proofs. An ordering-impotence theorem (Theorem 1: no fixed ordering escapes the first-order rate on collision-free families); antithetic cancellation at identical gate budget (Theorem 2); a quantitative BCH remainder (Theorem 3); strict optimality of leading cancellation (Theorem 4); NP-hardness of residual minimization with a spectral relaxation (Theorem 5); and an unbiased shot-based estimator of the residual with a finite-sample certified confidence interval (Theorem 6). Because a quantum computer is interrogated only through Born-rule samples, the accuracy claim itself is delivered as a certified statistical inference.
  3. A zero-overhead algorithm, released as a pip-installable package. CGS combines the antithetic fold with commuting-clique compression (Proposition 2) and an interpretable learned scheduler over ten listing-order-invariant graph features (Proposition 3), packaged as specops-cgs with the single reproduction command cgs-reproduce.
  4. A matched-cost empirical benchmark. Convergence rate 1.96 → 4.07 at identical rotation count; fidelity 0.99 reached with 1.40× fewer rotations (315 → 236); 0.999 reached only by the folded schedules (315 rotations) while no first-order ordering succeeds within the swept budget; size-independent order doubling up to n = 14 qubits with a 2.06× matched-cost speedup at n = 14; learned scheduler at 93% leave-one-out accuracy, near-oracle efficiency (114.0 vs. 112.4 mean rotations).

Reproduce every result

Every figure, table, and quoted number in the manuscript is generated from a single seeded run of the accompanying package; nothing is entered by hand. results/summary.json records full provenance (seed, command, platform, package versions, runtime, peak memory), and three integrity gates (fast Pauli kernel vs. dense expm, matrix-free error form vs. dense pair-commutator sum, Krylov reference vs. dense expm, all to 10−9) must pass before any number is emitted.

Context

This work is part of a research program on the spectral truncation of operators for query-efficient quantum optimization and simulation, in which statistics is treated as structural to quantum computing (Born-rule sampling makes every reported quantity an estimation problem) and interpretable learned surrogates are used to accelerate scientific computation. The manuscript is prepared for submission to PRX Quantum (REVTeX 4.2).
